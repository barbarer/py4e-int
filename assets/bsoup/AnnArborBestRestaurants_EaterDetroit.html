Ann Arbor’s Best Restaurants

Must-visit classic and contemporary dining destinations in Tree Town

by Mary Hayes Weier
Updated Oct 21, 2025, 4:05 PM UTC
Mary Hayes Weier is an award-winning writer and journalist who covers the local food scene from her home base in Ann Arbor, Michigan.

Home to the prestigious University of Michigan, Ann Arbor draws students and educators from all over the world. Every weekend its downtown sidewalks are dotted with visiting family members, and thousands more visitors pour into “A2” for summer festivals and raucous college football Saturdays. One constant is Ann Arbor’s community of proudly self-described “townies,” many of whom are more passionate about the university’s sports teams than the ever-revolving student population.

Not surprisingly, Ann Arbor’s diverse and cyclical nature influences its food scene. A decades-old pub known for its hearty burgers and chili might share sidewalk space with a swanky eatery that constructs its yellowtail crudo using tweezers and microgreens. A bakery might arrange its mung bean paste buns in the same case as its pistachio cannoli and chocolate chip cookies. And there are plenty of places to grab a local beer and watch some sports — unless slipping into a sleek dinner club for world-class live jazz and elevated cocktails is the evening’s goal.

All this variety makes it challenging to narrow down Ann Arbor’s best 13 restaurants, but choices had to be made. What follows are restaurants that consistently serve up quality food and are loved by locals and visitors alike. For newer additions to the dining scene check out the Ann Arbor Heatmap.

New to the map: In this update, we’re swapping out Aventura for its sister restaurant, Sava’s, which brings fresh new takes to the menu. We’re also bidding adieu to Krazy Jim’s Blimpy Burger and York Food and Drink, making room for two Main Street favorites: the enduring Black Pearl and the dazzling newcomer, Echelon.

Sava’s

Sava’s restaurant near the University of Michigan main campus continues to impress as one of Ann Arbor’s most polished dining experiences. Recent additions like the smoked whitefish toast and pork-and-kimchi nachos bring fresh energy to the menu while complementing longtime favorites. Their mac and cheese remains a standout — made with cheddar, jack, smoky Gouda, and Gruyere and topped with toasted breadcrumbs, it’s one of the best in town. With a range of price points served in an elegant, airy space, Sava strikes a balance of accessibility and sophistication. It also has one of the best brunch menus in Ann Arbor. Book your reservation on Tock.

Knight’s Steakhouse

Owned and run by the Knight family since 1984, this steakhouse is an Ann Arbor classic. It’s old-school Midwestern all the way and often packed — particularly the night before a game day. Good bets include steaks (filet mignon, New York strip, and the 22-ounce “Cowboy Steak”), a French onion soup that stands up to any Parisian bistro version, and a generously poured Manhattan. (Note: Knight’s Steakhouse Downtown, opened 30 years after the original Knight’s, has a similar menu but a more modern vibe, including comfy white leather seats that pull up to a massive, horseshoe-shaped bar.) Reservations can be booked on Resy.

Mani Osteria and Bar

Mani is the place to go in Ann Arbor for wood-fired oven pizzas (served on top of empty San Marzano tomato cans), pasta made from scratch, and seasonal soups and salads. Opened in 2011 by restaurateur Adam Baru, Mani’s isn’t old-school Italian American; its food is creative and bursting with innovative flavors. You’ll find creations like cauliflower fritti with pickled chile and bacon jam and a pizza with pistachios, goat cheese, and rosemary. A Caesar salad comes topped with a breaded and fried egg that spills golden yolk onto perfectly dressed romaine when cracked open. Still, Mani does the traditional dishes extraordinarily well, including Bolognese sauce folded into fresh pappardelle pasta and, of course, pepperoni pizza. Reservations are on OpenTable; takeout is available.

Miss Kim

Since opening the doors of her eponymous restaurant in 2016, chef Ji Hye Kim has gained national acclaim with five semifinalist nods from the James Beard Awards, including Best Chef: the Great Lakes Region in 2025. Kim’s restaurant is both cozy and modern, and menu highlights include her craveable Korean fried chicken (available in chile glaze, soy glaze, or a Korean spice dry rub), a rich soy butter rice, and broccolini tossed with anchovy caramel, crunchy cashews, and cilantro. Reservations are accepted through Resy, and there’s also a to-go option. For more casual noshing, check out Kim’s delicious new vegetarian restaurant right next door, Little Kim. Can’t-miss menu items include the fried tofu sandwich with red cabbage and seaweed mayo, and the paneer tomato sandwich with cilantro chutney on Zingerman’s Bakehouse’s slightly tangy farm bread.

Spencer

Warm and cozy with family-style seating, Spencer feels like you were invited to your cool chef-friend’s house for a memorable meal — which it will be. Chef and co-owner Abby Olitzky creates a new, set-priced menu with two entree choices for each calendar month, plus selected appetizers and dessert, posted on Spencer’s website (online reservations open up on Tock a few days before the end of each month for the following month and no walk-ins are accepted). Recent menus have featured options such as sweet corn agnolotti with sauerkraut and hazelnut, and pistachio plum galette with berry ripple ice cream. Be sure to dive into Spencer’s carefully curated wine list; it received a James Beard Award semifinalist nod for Outstanding Wine and Other Beverages Program in 2025 — its third year in a row.

  • (734) 369-3979

Echelon Kitchen & Bar

Since opening in February 2025, Echelon has graduated from “best new restaurant” buzz to one of Ann Arbor’s top fine-dining destinations. The airy dining room showcases an open kitchen with a wood-fired oven and custom wallpaper depicting local landmarks. Chef Joseph VanWagner — trained in Michelin-starred kitchens in Chicago and New York — has created a menu featuring refined entrees and delicious pastas. But where Echelon really shines is in its vegetable-forward focus, such as wood-fired cauliflower with tahini and golden raisins, or a selection of perfectly seasoned fresh vegetables (which change with the season) served on herbed, house-cultured crème fraiche. But as far as smash burgers go, you can’t beat the one served from the bar menu in its beautiful bar space overlooking Main Street. Cocktails like the martini-inspired Brine or the bourbon-based Modern Medicine (served tableside from a custom dispenser) round out the experience; book your table through Tock.

Black Pearl

Black Pearl has one of the best locations in the city, right on the corner of Main and William, making it a prime spot for outdoor dining in Ann Arbor when the weather is warm. The ever-popular fish tacos remain a staple, but the restaurant has expanded its offerings with a creative sushi menu. A standout is the ambitious black pearl roll — coconut shrimp tempura, avocado, and cucumber topped with torched hamachi, tropical and eel sauces, strawberries, and micro rainbow greens. (Yes, that’s a mouthful, both literally and figuratively.) With its lively atmosphere, inventive menu, and a recent interior expansion that now includes a massive horseshoe shaped bar, it’s a downtown favorite that keeps evolving. Reservations suggested.

Blue LLama Jazz Club

Blue Llama is a stunning experience, with crushed suede seating, midnight blue lighting, and a ceiling studded with tiny lights that a meal here feels like eating under the stars. Chef JJ Johnson, winner of the James Beard Foundation 2019 Book Award for American cuisine, brought his national shine and knowledge of Afro-Caribbean cuisine to the menu in 2024. Small plates include Harlem-style cornbread with sweet potato and peppa butter, and salmon croquettes with a black-eyed pea salad. From the large plates you might choose the bracingly spicy half jerk chicken with mango chow or a New York steak with peppercorn sauce and yuzu bell pepper slaw. Best of all, while you’re digging into all this great food, you get to enjoy entertainment by some of the best jazz musicians and vocalists in the U.S. The restaurant does accept walk-ins, but it’s wise to reserve a ticket online.

  • 314 S Main St, Ann Arbor, MI, 48104, US

Frita Batidos Ann Arbor

Chef-owner Eve Aranoff’s Frita Batidos, inspired by Miami’s Cuban street food culture, is wildly popular among students — and seemingly the first place they want to bring their visiting parents. It all revolves around the frita sandwich that’s ordered with your patty of choice, such as the classic chorizo, ground chicken, or black bean. Don’t miss the crispy “loaded” plantains topped with black beans, Muenster cheese, cilantro-lime salsa, avocado, and creme fraiche — and save room for the warm, nutmeg-scented churros with a side of chocolate sauce. Place orders for takeout online.

  • 117 West Washington Street, Ann Arbor, Michigan 48104
  • (734) 761-2882

Peridot

Peridot, which opened in 2023, has established itself as a night-out destination for high-end, Vietnamese-influenced cuisine, creative cocktails, and a chic atmosphere, complete with mood lighting and a contemporary soundtrack. Peridot’s version of the classic green papaya salad comes with salty slivers of beef jerky, and the tangy ketchup-fried rice, inspired by one of the executive chefs and owner Duc Tang’s beloved childhood dishes, does indeed spark childlike glee. The grilled seabream with rice noodles, nuoc cham, and lettuce leaves for eating wrap-style is the perfect dish for sharing with a dining partner. Book a table on Tock.

Chela’s Mexican Restaurant

This family-owned casual restaurant enjoys a strong following among both locals and students, featuring fresh Mexican street food with roots in Mexico City. You’ll find the classic carne asada taco, simply prepared with cilantro and chopped white onion and folded into soft, warm corn tortillas. Other Mexican favorites are also available, including build-your-own burritos and a variety of savory and sweet tamales. Chela’s makes three different types of homemade salsas of varying heat — all fantastic. Wash it all down with a bottle of Jarritos fruit soda or a Mexican Coca-Cola. Dine in or place an order for pickup or delivery.

  • 693 S Maple Rd, Ann Arbor, MI, 48103, US

Zingerman’s Roadhouse

This restaurant’s tagline is “really good American food,” and we concur. The Roadhouse sources its produce and cheeses from local farms and artisan creameries, resulting in exceptional quality. There are many nods to Southern cooking — including house-smoked meats and owner Ari Weinzweig’s pimento cheese spread. Other good bets include thoughtfully constructed salads, a rotating selection of fresh oysters, and an unforgettable mac and cheese tossed with spit-roasted smoked chicken, corn, and green chiles — all of which are, indeed, “really good.” It’s no wonder, the James Beard Awards committee gave serious consideration to Zingerman’s Roadhouse in 2019. Reservations are available through OpenTable.

Star’s Cafe

Tucked in a strip mall, this family-owned Middle Eastern restaurant is always busy with regulars at lunch or early dinner (they close at 7 p.m.). There are tables for eating in but for many, it’s grab-and-go. In fact, it’s the kind of place that you’ll find an excuse to drive by so you can pick up a warm, foil-wrapped pita sandwich stuffed with falafel, tabbouleh, tahini, and pickles, or one with chicken shawarma and garlic sauce (both can be ordered “spicy” with chile sauce, too). Other winners include a well-made hummus and a soothing crushed lentil soup.

  • 2575 Jackson Ave, Ann Arbor, MI, 48103, US
  • (734) 996-0299
